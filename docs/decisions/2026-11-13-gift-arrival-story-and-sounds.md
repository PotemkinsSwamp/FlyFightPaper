# A gift's arrival: the board's plane brings it, you open it, music turns right down

**Decided:** 2026-11-13 (Alan, from his [playtest notes](../playtests/2026-11-13-gift-a-friend.md) and a second look
in Studio the same day; his last word: "Yeah, that's it.")
**Status:** built (Studio only until go-live). How it plays: [the gift arriving](../systems/economy-and-shop.md#the-gift-arriving).

## Decision
- **His story for the moment:** the menu board's own pinned plane leaves its pin, flies right, turns and comes at you,
  goes off the top; the box falls in; you open it; each Thanks! brings the next gift out of the open box; after the
  last one the plane is pinned again. No stray star. The rays move in and out in turns.
- **A little wait before each gift:** the box rustles and shakes, then the gift pops out ("you have to rustle and find
  it and pull it out").
- **Sounds, picked by ear:** the shine while a gift sits on its rays (shine 2 of three: "a radiating sound, something
  shiny, brand new") and the rustle (rustle 2 of three). **No ding or coin** here ("too strong").
- **Music turns down to a tenth** under a moment like this ("just like the background is dimmed"; a fifth was still
  too loud). Claude applied the same to the unlock backpack (his "whenever an animation pops up like this").
- **No menu song of its own** (he raised it, then dropped it: "perhaps not").

## Why
A gift should feel like opening a present: you see who it's from, you open it, you dig it out, it shines. The game's
music was fighting the moment's own sounds.

## Not settled
- The pop is the Point Popper's balloon pop played higher and soft (no licensed cartoon or cork pop was found in
  Roblox's library); Alan heard it and didn't remark on it.
- Not checked on a phone size, nor with a real gift sent through the shop.
